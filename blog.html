5 Myths About
Lifting Heavy

You won't get "too bulky," you will get stronger, leaner, and more confident. Let's kill these myths once and for all.

One of the most common concerns I hear from new clients — especially women — is: "I don't want to get bulky." This myth, along with several others, has kept countless people from the training style that would benefit them most. Let's break them down.

Myth 1: "Lifting heavy will make me bulky."

Building significant muscle mass requires a caloric surplus, years of dedicated training, and in many cases, hormonal advantages most people don't have. Lifting heavy weights while eating at maintenance or a deficit will make you leaner and more toned — not bulky.

Myth 2: "Cardio burns more fat than weights."

Cardio burns calories during exercise. Strength training burns calories during exercise AND raises your resting metabolic rate for up to 48 hours post-workout through muscle repair. More muscle = higher metabolism = more fat burned around the clock.

Myth 3: "I need to lift every day to see results."

Muscles grow during rest, not during training. Training breaks muscle tissue down — recovery builds it back stronger. For most people, 3–4 days of strength training per week with adequate sleep and nutrition produces optimal results.

Myth 4: "Light weights with high reps tone better than heavy weights."

Both approaches can build muscle if taken close to failure. But progressive overload — gradually increasing the challenge — is the key driver of muscle development. Don't be afraid to push heavier over time.

Myth 5: "Soreness means a good workout."

Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS) is caused by novel stimulus, not workout quality. As you adapt to training, soreness decreases — that's progress, not regression. Judge your workouts by performance improvements, not pain level.

Bottom line: Lift heavy, recover well, eat enough protein, and repeat consistently. The results will speak for themselves.

How to Stay
Consistent with Fitness

Motivation gets you started — systems keep you going. Here are 7 strategies I give every new client to make working out a non-negotiable habit, not a chore.

  1. Schedule it like a meeting. Put your workouts in your calendar and treat them as immovable.
  2. Start smaller than you think. A 20-min workout you do beats a 90-min workout you skip.
  3. Track your progress. Seeing your numbers go up is addictive in the best possible way.
  4. Find a training partner or coach. Accountability dramatically increases consistency.
  5. Prepare your environment. Lay out your gym clothes the night before. Remove friction.
  6. Focus on identity, not outcomes. "I am someone who exercises" vs. "I want to lose 20 lbs."
  7. Allow imperfect days. One bad session doesn't erase your progress. Just show up again.
